# Myanmar Economic Downward Spiral — Evidence Verification Matrix v1.0

## Purpose
Turn the causal-risk framework into a testable evidence matrix. This document does not assert that all proposed crisis stages are occurring. Populate every row with dated, source-attributed evidence before making current-state claims.

## Evidence rules
- Keep **observed data**, **interpretation**, and **forecast** separate.
- Record publication date and period measured; never confuse nominal values with real values.
- Prefer primary official data and reputable independent analysis; triangulate where data are incomplete.
- A single anecdote is a case example, not proof of a nationwide trend.
- Use “unknown / unavailable” rather than inventing a value.
- Do not infer hyperinflation or sovereign fiscal collapse from price rises alone.

## Indicator matrix

| Theme | Indicator to collect | Why it matters | Quality check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input costs | LPG/gas, electricity, fuel, freight and imported-input prices | Tests cost-push pressure | Same unit, geography and time period |
| Inflation | Headline and food CPI; month-on-month and year-on-year | Measures price changes | Source, basket and method disclosed |
| Currency | Market exchange rate, official rate, spread and volatility | Tracks import-cost and confidence pressure | Distinguish quoted rates and transaction rates |
| Household welfare | Real wages, food affordability, household consumption | Tests purchasing-power loss | Deflate nominal income; disclose limitations |
| Business activity | Sales volume, capacity utilisation, closure/opening counts | Tests margin squeeze and demand destruction | Sector and sample coverage stated |
| Labour market | Employment, unemployment/underemployment, wages and hours | Tests job-market deterioration | Explain survey coverage and informal-sector limits |
| External sector | Exports, foreign-exchange receipts, reserves, import availability | Tests supply and external financing constraints | Period, valuation and coverage consistent |
| Public finance | Revenue, spending, deficit, financing mix, arrears and debt service | Tests fiscal stress | Nominal and real figures separated |
| Monetary conditions | Monetary aggregates and central-bank claims on government | Tests monetary-financing pressure | Avoid assuming causation from correlation alone |
| Essential services | Availability and prices of food, medicine, fuel and electricity | Tracks real-world impact | Location and observation date recorded |

## Decision gates
**Gate A — Cost pressure:** multiple dated input-price series corroborate the change.
**Gate B — Demand and firm distress:** evidence shows real sales or consumption weakening and business distress increasing.
**Gate C — Labour and fiscal feedback:** employment/income and real revenue evidence show deterioration; distinguish nominal tax growth from real growth.
**Gate D — Monetary/fiscal stress:** documented financing and price data support a credible risk assessment.
**Gate E — Extreme-outcome labels:** apply “hyperinflation” or “sovereign fiscal crisis” only when transparent definitions and reliable evidence support them.
