# Laphet Pilot Protocol — Field and Publication Gates

## Before any contact or collection

1. Identify a willing Laphet-producing or practicing community partner and a safe, appropriate contact route.
2. Provide a plain-language explanation in the preferred language: purpose, exact scope, intended audience, storage, retention, withdrawal, risks, benefits and who will review the result.
3. Agree who is authorized to speak for the community and which decisions require individual contributor approval.
4. Agree what must not be recorded, photographed, translated, uploaded or published.
5. Do not collect until consent for the specific activity is recorded.

## Consent matrix

| Activity | Separate permission |
|---|---|
| Interview / oral history | Yes |
| Name and attribution | Yes; anonymous/community-only remains available |
| Photograph / video / audio | Yes, per media scope |
| Recipe and preparation detail | Yes |
| Researcher access | Yes, with purpose |
| Public web publication | Yes, for exact scope |
| Translation | Yes, per language/use |
| Commercial licensing/reuse | Separate agreement |
| Retention and future reuse | Explicit period and purpose |
| Withdrawal | Explain process and practical limits before collection |

Consent is not a single checkbox. Record the version of the explanation, date, language, authorized signatory/participant, exact scopes, review date, and withdrawal path.

## Minimum record intake

Collect only what is authorized:
- local food name(s), script and pronunciation;
- community-defined description and context;
- optional region at the precision approved by participants;
- documented preparation variant(s), not a single imposed canonical recipe;
- ingredient list, if approved;
- process observations, if approved;
- media references and rights evidence;
- claim-to-source links and limitations;
- contributor attribution preference;
- access classification and retention rule.

Unknown origin, disputed history and differences in practice must remain visible as such.

## Scientific review

The 2020 microbiology paper is contextual research, not a safety standard. If further tests are proposed, first document permissions and a qualified protocol. Preserve batch identity, sample collection/transport/storage, chain of custody, laboratory identity, method, calibration where applicable, result, uncertainty and reviewer interpretation. Never infer a safe batch from pH alone.

A food-safety or nutrition claim must not be published unless its own evidence and qualified review support that specific claim. Health/medical claims are out of scope for this pilot.

## Media integrity

For each image/audio/video item record:
- original file identifier and SHA-256;
- actual subject depicted and capture date if known;
- contributor/creator;
- usage scope and permission evidence;
- any edits/crops/translations;
- whether the visual genuinely depicts the described Laphet or practice.

AI-generated or illustrative images must never be passed off as documentary evidence.

## Review and release

1. Cultural reviewers check names, terminology, context, attribution, variations and misrepresentation risk.
2. Qualified scientific reviewers check the scientific statements within their competence.
3. Independent QA checks claims, evidence, media rights, translation, safety wording and conflicts of interest.
4. Present the exact version to the authorized community reviewers.
5. Record the exact artifact SHA-256, approved languages, channels, date, and approver authority.
6. Release only that exact version. Any material change requires a new review/approval.
7. Preserve correction, takedown and withdrawal procedures.

## Fail-closed conditions

- missing/withdrawn/expired consent;
- no clear authority to approve;
- unresolved dispute presented as settled fact;
- restricted evidence used to support public claims;
- media permission absent;
- safety or health claims without qualified evidence;
- scientific review or independent QA not passed;
- community approval missing or artifact hash mismatch;
- channel/language is outside the consent scope.

Any such condition results in HOLD, not an inferred PASS.
