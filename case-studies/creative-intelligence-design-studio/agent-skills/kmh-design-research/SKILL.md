# KMH Design Research

1. Restate the design problem, target users, context, and desired outcome.
2. Separate observed evidence, user-provided facts, assumptions, and open questions.
3. Identify child-safety, privacy, accessibility, localization, and cultural constraints where relevant.
4. Produce research questions, participant criteria, task flows, and success measures; do not invent research findings or participant quotes.
5. Map findings to product requirements and cite the actual source for each material statement.
6. Provide at least two design directions only when the brief benefits from alternatives; explain trade-offs.
7. Record unresolved evidence gaps as `PENDING`, not as inferred facts.

## Output contract

Return: problem statement, evidence/assumption ledger, user needs, constraints, journey or flow, prioritized requirements, risks, and next validation method.

## Guardrails

Do not collect children's direct identifiers. Do not claim a usability study happened unless sessions and evidence exist. Do not copy another brand's proprietary identity; use references to reason about patterns, not to impersonate.
